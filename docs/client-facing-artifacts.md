# Client-Facing Artifact Method

Use this method to turn approved source material into a client-facing proposal,
completion report, statement of work, or project update without losing facts
or writing from the internal team's point of view.

## Source Boundary

Create a short source ledger before drafting. Record:

- confirmed facts;
- agreed scope and outcomes;
- assumptions;
- approval gates;
- unresolved decisions; and
- language that must remain exact.

Do not use the rewrite to settle an open decision or strengthen a tentative
statement.

## Drafting Method

For each workstream, answer three questions:

1. **What is the workstream?** Use a name the client can recognize.
2. **What does it cover?** Explain the work in plain operating language.
3. **What is the agreed outcome?** Name the person or team, what they can do,
   the concrete output or changed state, how they verify it, and any safeguard
   that makes the result trustworthy.

Use artifacts as evidence or support. Avoid making an artifact inventory carry
the entire outcome.

## Three-Pass Review

### 1. Factual Preservation

- Trace each material claim to the source ledger.
- Confirm names, roles, dates, amounts, providers, and scope boundaries.
- Keep assumptions and approval gates visible.
- Remove invented behavior, proof, or commitments.

### 2. Client Usefulness

- Confirm the reader can see what changes in the working day.
- Name who acts and what they can do.
- Name what the client receives, sees, or verifies.
- Make the requested decision or next step clear.
- Remove internal planning fields that do not help the client.

### 3. Voice Fit

- Prefer the client's vocabulary over internal abstractions.
- Keep the tone direct, specific, and appropriately confident.
- Remove consultant polish, generic sales language, and agent or repository
  narration.
- Read the outcome aloud. It should sound like a person explaining the work to
  the client, not a system describing its own process.

The assigned writer or reviewer can complete all three passes. A separate
owner or client review is a task-specific approval choice, not a default
definition-of-done requirement for this method.

## Completion Record

For autonomous work, leave a short review record that names:

- the artifact and source set reviewed;
- factual-preservation checks completed;
- client-usefulness findings;
- voice-fit findings;
- changes made or recommended;
- remaining assumptions or decisions; and
- automated checks that ran.

This record supports accountability. It does not turn automated checks into a
claim that the document is factually correct or ready for signature.
