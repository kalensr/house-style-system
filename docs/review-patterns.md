# Review Patterns For Direct Public Writing

This guide turns recurring editorial feedback into reusable review prompts. It
uses synthetic examples so that the public House Style System can improve
drafting without storing private conversations or source material.

## State The Positive Claim First

An opening that explains what an idea is not makes the reader carry an
unnecessary comparison. State the useful claim first and then give the reader
the evidence or operating consequence.

Before:

```text
The value is not a borrowed playbook. It is a broader perspective.
```

After:

```text
Experience across settings gives leaders a broader perspective for evaluating a problem.
```

## Let Meaning Set The Rhythm

Parallel sentence openings and serial questions can make a draft sound written
for cadence. Questions belong when the author is genuinely inviting inquiry.
When related details describe one operating reality, combine them into clear
prose.

Before:

```text
Where does the information come from? Who makes the decision? Which system handles the step?
```

After:

```text
Leaders need a clear picture of the information entering the process, the people responsible for decisions, and the systems handling repeatable steps.
```

## Keep The Actor In View

Phrases such as `the work can stay connected` make an abstraction carry the
sentence. Name a person or team. Name the system, decision, or workflow the
reader can observe.

Before:

```text
The work can stay connected to both sides of the problem.
```

After:

```text
Technology leaders can work with executives setting direction and teams responsible for delivery.
```

## Preserve The Intended Career Scope

Public writing should describe the leadership scope the author intends to
communicate. A specific engagement model belongs in a piece when it is the
subject of that piece. Broader leadership positioning should remain broad.

Before:

```text
Fractional technology leadership is where I focus.
```

After:

```text
I am drawn to technology leadership roles where I can work with executives on direction and with technology teams on delivery.
```

## Review Sequence

1. Read the draft for the central claim and the concrete operating situation.
2. Replace negation-first framing with a direct statement of the claim.
3. Read the paragraph aloud for repeated openings and question chains.
4. Name the actor whenever an abstraction begins carrying the sentence.
5. Confirm that career language reflects the author’s intended scope.
6. Use the style gate for repeatable signals and human review for rhythm,
   meaning, evidence, and final fit.
