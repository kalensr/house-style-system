# Sloganized Synthesis Pairs

These synthetic pairs exercise the four natural-voice review prompts. The
first passage in each pair is a nomination, not proof of poor authorship. The
revision shows the concrete information that the polished formula hid.

## Abstract causal formula

Before: Customer trust is downstream of leadership alignment.

After: The service manager reviews unresolved complaints with the regional
leaders each Friday. They decide who will contact each customer and record the
commitment in the case file.

## Sloganized parallelism

Before: Changing the slide rarely changes the decision.

After: The leadership team kept the proposal after the presenter revised the
slides because the budget assumptions and approval request stayed the same.

## Depersonalized case narration

Before: A consultant was hired to improve an equipment company’s sales story.

After: The company’s owner asked the consultant to review why distributors
were declining the new product. Sales managers then showed where buyers were
rejecting the maintenance terms.

## Authoritative solution label

Before: The fix was three decisions: simplify the offer, train the team, and
track the response.

After: The owner removed one service tier after customers confused it with the
standard plan. She then trained the sales team on the remaining difference and
tracked which questions customers still asked. The source does not yet show
whether those changes improved sales.
